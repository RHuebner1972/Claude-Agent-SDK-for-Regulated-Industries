# AML-SOP-012: Alert Disposition and SAR Escalation (synthetic, for course use)

Bayside Community Bank. Applies to transaction-monitoring alerts handled with the AML triage assistant.

1. The analyst who works an alert, and any assistant acting for that analyst, may read alerts and customer data, add case notes and **request** actions. They never approve their own requests.
2. Closing an alert (false positive or not suspicious) needs approval by an **AML senior analyst or the BSA Officer** who did not work the alert.
3. A SAR recommendation needs **two approvals by two different people**, one of whom is the **BSA Officer**. The SAR itself must be filed no later than 30 calendar days after initial detection (31 CFR 1020.320(b)(3)), so approval requests carry a due time well inside that window.
4. Account restrictions (blocking outgoing wires, freezing) need approval by the **BSA Officer**.
5. Approvers with a business relationship to the customer (for example, as relationship manager) may not approve actions on that customer's alerts.
6. A request that is not decided by its due time **escalates** to the Chief Compliance Officer. A second miss **expires** the request. Nothing is ever approved by default.
7. Every approval is an electronic signature: the signer's name, the date and time, and the meaning of the signature, linked to the exact action approved.
8. SARs and anything that would reveal a SAR are confidential (31 CFR 1020.320(e)). The assistant has no tool to contact customers.
